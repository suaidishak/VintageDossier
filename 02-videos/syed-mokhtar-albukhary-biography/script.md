# Script: Syed Mokhtar Albukhary - From Two Lorries to an Empire
Format 9:16 · target 2:00 · ~300 words (±5 %) · chapter headers are NOT read aloud

## Chapter 1 - The Cattle Boy
Two lorries. That was all a young man from Kedah could afford.
But his permits said four.
His name is Syed Mokhtar Albukhary. And those lorries would carry him to the top of Malaysia's rich list.
He was born on the 12th of December 1951, in a small village near Alor Setar.
His family traced its roots to Hadhramaut, in Yemen.
As a schoolboy, he helped his father in the cattle trade.
Then foot-and-mouth disease struck, and the family business collapsed.
He left school early.
He traded rice, and with a government loan, he started a lorry business.
By thirty, he was a millionaire.

## Chapter 2 - The Empire
In the 1990s, Malaysia began selling state assets to private owners.
Syed Mokhtar moved fast.
Ports. Power plants. An airport. The national rice importer, Bernas.
Later came Proton, Pos Malaysia, and a bank.
His empire now touches daily life: the rice Malaysians eat, the letters they send, the cars they drive.
His companies run Tanjung Pelepas, one of the biggest container ports in Southeast Asia.
Forbes puts his fortune at more than two billion US dollars.
Each deal made his group bigger, and its debts larger.
Yet he almost never gives interviews.

## Chapter 3 - Two Faces
Critics say his rise was helped by close ties to Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad.
In 2022, Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim said he had reprimanded him over the rice monopoly.
Mahathir says he rescued struggling companies. His supporters point to what he gave away.
In 1976, he pledged half his salary to fifteen poor families.
In 2014, Forbes Asia named him a Hero of Philanthropy.
Today his foundation runs a free university for poor students from around the world.
The boy who left school early now pays for others to finish theirs.
