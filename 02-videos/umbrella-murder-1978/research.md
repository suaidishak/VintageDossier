# Research: The Umbrella Murder on Waterloo Bridge (1978)

## Summary
Georgi Markov, a Bulgarian novelist and playwright, defected to the West in 1969 and settled in London.
He worked for the BBC World Service Bulgarian section, and from 1975 his Radio Free Europe series
"In Absentia Reports" mocked the regime of party leader Todor Zhivkov from the inside.
On 7 September 1978 (Zhivkov's birthday) he felt a sharp jab in his right thigh at a bus stop by Waterloo Bridge.
A man picked up an umbrella, apologised in a foreign accent and left in a taxi.
Markov developed a high fever and died in St James' Hospital, Balham, on 11 September 1978, aged 49.
The post-mortem found a tiny platinum-iridium pellet (~1.5 mm) with two drilled holes; Porton Down concluded ricin.
Weeks earlier another exile, Vladimir Kostov, had been hit in the Paris Metro; his pellet was identical and he survived.
Bulgaria's secret service (DS) is widely held responsible, with KGB technical help (ex-KGB general Oleg Kalugin).
In 1990 Bulgarian files were destroyed; two generals were convicted of it. Suspect: Francesco Gullino, "Agent Piccadilly".
Nobody was ever charged. Bulgaria closed its case in 2013 (statute of limitations); Gullino died in 2021.

## Numbers
- Pellet: ~1.52 mm wide, platinum 90% / iridium 10%, two 0.35 mm holes (holes: verify) - PBS, Bristol Univ. project
- Ricin dose: ~0.2 mg (CDC slide) - estimates vary
- Time from jab to death: 4 days (7 → 11 Sep)
- Age at death: 49
- Files destroyed in 1990: ~140,000 (Novinite)
- Sentence in absentia: 6 yr 6 mo

## Disputed / uncertain
- Weapon: an umbrella-gun (Kalugin, popular view) vs. the umbrella only as a distraction while a separate device fired the pellet.
- Hospital admission: evening of 7 Sep vs. morning of 8 Sep.
- Pellet size: 1.52 / 1.53 / 1.7 mm across sources.
- Kostov attack date: 26, 27 or 28 August 1978; station named Étoile or Arc de Triomphe (same place).
- Killer: Gullino ("Piccadilly") per DS files and The Times; another theory names an agent "The Woodpecker" (Klaus Dexel). No proof either way.
- Order: Kalugin says Zhivkov ordered it; no written Bulgarian order has ever surfaced.
- BBC start: 1971 vs 1972. Birthday timing deliberate? Unproven.
