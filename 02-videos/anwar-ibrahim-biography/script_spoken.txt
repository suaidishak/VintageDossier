September nineteen ninety-eight. Malaysia's former Deputy Prime Minister walks into court with a swollen black eye.
Weeks earlier, he was the man expected to run the country.
His name is Anwar Ibrahim. And his fall was only the beginning.

He was born on the tenth of August nineteen forty-seven, in a small village in Penang.
As a student, he led Muslim youth groups and became a fierce voice for the poor.
In nineteen seventy-four, he stood with students protesting hunger among rubber tappers in Baling.
He was detained without trial for twenty months.
Then, in nineteen eighty-two, a surprise. Prime Minister Mahathir Mohamad invited him into the ruling party, UMNO.
Anwar rose fast. Finance Minister in nineteen ninety-one. Deputy Prime Minister in nineteen ninety-three.
Everyone saw him as Mahathir's heir.

Then the Asian financial crisis hit, and the two men split.
On the second of September nineteen ninety-eight, Mahathir sacked him.
Anwar took his fight to the streets. The crowds called it Reformasi.
Weeks later, he was arrested and beaten in police custody.
Courts convicted him of corruption, then sodomy.
He called the charges political. So did international rights groups.
In two thousand and four, the top court overturned the sodomy verdict, and he walked free after six years.
In two thousand and eight, he won back his old seat and led the opposition.
But in twenty fifteen, a second sodomy conviction sent him back to prison.

Then, in May twenty eighteen, a twist.
His coalition won the election, allied with his old rival, Mahathir.
The King granted Anwar a full pardon.
Four years later, a new election left no party with a majority.
On the twenty-fourth of November twenty twenty-two, Anwar Ibrahim was sworn in as Malaysia's tenth Prime Minister.
He was seventy-five years old.
Twenty-four years after the black eye, the prisoner had become the Prime Minister.
